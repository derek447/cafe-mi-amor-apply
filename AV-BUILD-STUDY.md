# Two Loose Screws — A/V Build Location Study
**2000 Regal 2760 Commodore · HIN RGMRC083C000 · (renamed 2860 Commodore 2002-2005 — same hull; search and request docs under BOTH names)**

## 1. Schematics & Manuals

### What exists (and what doesn't)
There is **no owner-downloadable factory wiring schematic** for 1999-2003 Regal Commodores anywhere online. Regal never published standalone electrical drawings for this era; detailed harness documentation lives behind the **dealer-only Regal Dealer Support portal** (regalboats.zendesk.com). What *does* exist:

- **Era owner's manuals with a Systems chapter** containing Regal's standard **wire color code chart** and a technical-drawing appendix (dash/forward/aft connector drawings; transom speaker wiring in later revisions). This chart is your de-facto tracing guide: **Yellow/Black 16 AWG = stereo memory; Red 16 = stereo remote/dash feed; Red 2/0 = main DC panel feed; Green 8 = bonding; Blue 14/10 = cabin lighting; Pink 16 = fuel sender.**
- The **correct-era revision** for your MY2000 boat is the owner's manual covering **2660/2760/2960/3260, revised 11/1999**.
- Factory audio baseline: Kenwood head unit at the helm, optional Kenwood multi-disc changer; optional "stereo performance package" amp lived **under the helm or in the wet-bar cabinet on a 30A breaker** near the battery switch; stereo memory fuse near the battery switch or under the dash.
- Regal's old Canto manual library (regalboats.canto.com/s/J4HTN) is **dead**. regalboats.com/owners-resources has **no HIN lookup** and no Commodore-era PDFs.

### Exact process to request from Regal
1. **Email** customerservice@regalboats.com (alternates: customer.service@regalboats.com, regal@regalboats.com) or **call 407-851-4360 / 1-800-US-REGAL (1-800-877-3425)**, or use the regalboats.com contact form.
2. Give: **HIN RGMRC083C000, 2760 Commodore, MY2000** — and note it was later sold as the **2860 Commodore**.
3. Ask specifically for: the **owner's manual PDF (2660/2760/2960/3260 revision 11/1999)** *plus* the **"technical drawings" appendix pages and any wiring schematics** — DC panel, dash harness, stereo wiring.
4. **Caution:** a documented iboats case paid ~$49 and received the *wrong* manual — confirm the revision before paying. Owners report Regal responds quickly via the contact form.
5. **Backup route:** any Regal dealer's service department can pull electrical documentation from the dealer portal (the "Electrical – Components & Operation" article, #25460146644244, points to the technical diagrams in the back of each model manual).
6. Mail/records: Regal Marine Industries, Attn: Customer Service, 2300 Jetport Drive, Orlando FL 32809.

## 3. The Recommended Build Map

**Four stations, ~200-250 lb total, deliberately placed low, central, and port-of-center — this build can *improve* the boat's documented starboard list and stern-heavy trim if you follow the weight logic.**

### Station 1 — 18" sub: mid-cabin berth aft footwell, against the ER bulkhead, port-of-centerline, firing forward
All three lenses converge here; the disagreements were about *how*, and they resolve cleanly:

- **Volume & mass:** a 6 ft³ net ported box is ~8-9 ft³ external (~24×24×27" with 3/4" walls). This footwell is the only space aboard that takes it, and it sits essentially over the tanks near the LCG — 120-150 lb costs almost nothing in planing trim, and biasing port actively corrects the ~2" factory starboard lean (~150 lb = visible list on this hull).
- **Mounting (resolves acoustics vs. environment):** glassed-in cleats / epoxied 3/4" backer boards to the **hull sides and cabin face of the ER bulkhead** — *never* fasteners down into the berth decking (the 105-110-gal fuel tank is centered directly beneath) and *never* a cut through the ER bulkhead (it's the gasoline fume boundary). This satisfies the structure lens's rigidity demand and the survey lens's boundary rules simultaneously. A freestanding strapped box is the lazy fallback but sacrifices the whole berth; cleats preserve most of it.
- **Firing direction (resolves the "aft into cockpit" idea):** forward into the cabin. The cabin is a pressure vessel — strong cabin gain below ~40 Hz — and 30-35 Hz energy floods the cockpit through the companionway and camper canvas anyway. The electrical lens's step-riser port exit (into the owner-praised starboard step "boxed area") is a legitimate phase-2 *only after* bore-scoping the riser confirms it's clear of the fuel tank top, courtesy-light wiring, and is not part of the fume boundary. Don't gate the build on it.
- **Design targets:** 5.5-6.5 ft³ net, slot port 60-80 in² generously flared (a sleeper's head is feet from this port — chuffing at 2 kW is the audible failure), tune 30-33 Hz, internal window bracing every ~10", high-pass the cockpit/cabin Kenwoods at 80-100 Hz.
- **Access reality:** every source calls aft-cabin access "very tight." Assume a **two-piece bolted/gasketed enclosure or build-in-place through the removable forward wall panels** (screws hidden under the upholstery buttons) until the cardboard mockup proves otherwise.

### Station 2 — 2kW mono block + LiFePO4 bank: mid-berth aft bulkhead, beside the battery-switch white box
Unanimous across lenses, and where the disagreement over the battery's home resolves:

- The factory battery switches live in "the little white box at the back of the midships berth" — the berth headboard **is** the engine-room bulkhead. A mono block on an isolated backer board here gets a **3-6 ft 1/0 run** (<2% drop at ~200 A dynamic). The electrical lens's own math settles the settee-base-battery debate: **the high-current path length dominates**, so the battery goes where the amp is; the charger's low-current AC/DC runs can be long. If field measurement shows no room for a Group-31-class LiFePO4 beside the white box, fall back to the port settee base and upsize to 2/0.
- This is the **cabin side of the fume boundary**: no ignition-protection mandate, cooler than the ER (LiFePO4 charge ceiling ~45-55°C), and the ~40-70 lb lands low and central-to-port — exactly where this hull wants it. Direct precedent: the Sea Ray 290 Sundancer install (amp behind the mid-berth access panel on a 3/4" backer board epoxied to the hull).
- **Rules:** sealed/strapped battery tray; **Class-T fuse within 7" of the positive post** (ABYC E-9/E-13); BMS alarm brought to the helm; every cable penetration of the ER bulkhead through a proper sealed gland; electronics on **rubber-isolated standoffs on a separate backer** — never on the sub enclosure or a panel it loads (vibration co-location is the documented amp-killer). Thermostatic fan or louvered panel for the mono block in a sleeping cabin.
- **Charging:** leave the Charles 15A on the start banks; add a dedicated LiFePO4-profile shore charger on a new branch breaker at the galley AC panel (the covered-slip/shore-power life makes this trivial); optional DC-DC from the start bank for away-from-dock top-up.

### Station 3 — 4/5-channel amp: under the helm console
The factory put its optional amp exactly here on a 30A breaker; the old Kenwood head-unit location, all existing 6.5" speaker runs, and Regal's stereo circuits (Yellow/Black 16 memory, Red 16 remote) terminate here; and the documented **starboard-gunnel wiring chase** from the engine room lands here. Reuse the factory speaker runs after verifying gauge/condition. Its 4-2 AWG power feed comes up the chase (~10-14 ft) — **pull a messenger line while it's open** (owner-recommended). The mono block does **not** come here: the 200 A run is too long and the dash bakes. Mount high on a backer with drip loops (era walk-through windshields weep), standoffs, and ventilation; keep power leads away from the compass; put a **manual switch in the remote turn-on lead**. Add a small waterproof helm controller/remote screen mirroring the cabin media node.

### Station 4 — Touchscreen + media node: aft salon bulkhead (mid-berth front wall)
Unanimous. Fixes a documented owner annoyance (early boats had no fixed TV — it shuffled between forward berth and galley). Through-bolted VESA mount with a backing plate; **pull the buttoned panels and photograph everything behind before drilling** — the battery-switch box, harness, and cabin-lighting feeds (Blue 14/10) live in this structure. The media-node placement dispute resolves for the **cabin side, in the cavity behind/beside the screen**: short HDMI (1-2 m) beats a long run to the helm every time, and the node runs on a **12V buck converter off the LiFePO4 — the boat has no inverter, and keeping the whole entertainment chain DC avoids inverter hash and ground loops**. Audio drops to the helm amp via the forward-bilge route behind the mid-berth panels; USB touch alongside. Ventilate the node cavity; keep the screen out of the Origo stove's heat path and check settee sightlines/glare before committing.

### Wiring discipline (all stations)
Factory chases only — starboard gunnel chase for power/remote, forward-bilge route behind the mid-berth panels for signal. **Never bundle RCAs with the engine harness in the starboard chase** (alternator whine) — use twisted differential interconnects or route low-level on the opposite side. Fuse every positive at the source; grommets/glands at every bulkhead edge; conduit fill under ~40%; pull a spare messenger.

## 4. Red Flags & Rules

### Fuel & ignition protection (the non-negotiables)
- The **110-gal fiberglass fuel tank is centered directly under the cockpit sole and the mid-cabin aft decking**. Never blind-screw or cut the cockpit sole, the mid-berth decking, or anything above the tank without physically locating the tank top first (via the sender/inspection access).
- This is a **gasoline boat** (twin 4.3Gi + gas Kohler 5E). Under 33 CFR 183.410 / ABYC E-11, everything electrical in or vented to the engine/tank space must be **ignition-protected (SAE J1171)** — and essentially nothing in this build (B&C driver, amps, Pi, drop-in LiFePO4 BMS) carries that rating. **The entire build stays on the cabin/cockpit side of the fume boundaries.**
- A **ported enclosure may never share air with the engine bay**, and the **ER bulkhead is the fume boundary** — no cuts; cable penetrations only through sealed glands. This is what kills the aft-bench idea and any "fire aft through the berth headboard" scheme.
- The **clog-prone centerline drain channel between the fuel and waste tanks** runs exactly where the sub's weight lands. **Clear it, flow-test it, and keep it inspectable before building anything over it.**

### Structures not to cut
ER bulkhead (fume boundary) · cockpit sole and mid-berth decking (tank lid) · aft bench/engine-bay boundary · the molded wet-bar face (irreversible, and it's a wet zone anyway). The **wood stringers** have a good rot reputation for this vintage — keep it that way: moisture-meter before cutting anywhere, and **epoxy-seal every fastener hole into wood**.

### Weight & trim
~150 lb off-center produces a **visible list**, and the boat is **starboard-heavy from the factory** (galley, molded head, waste tank, water heater, both batteries). It's also **stern-weight-sensitive for planing** (owners move weight forward to get on plane). Rules: all added mass goes **port or centerline, low, and forward of the engine hatch**; nothing heavy aft of it. Placed per the build map, this ~200-250 lb build is corrective, not parasitic.

### Moisture
Era Regal **cockpit cabinetry rots** (documented rebuilds); the wet bar is a plumbing chase and condensation trap even at a covered slip — no electronics, ever. Drip loops and no gear under windshield seams at the helm. Expect condensation on a big screen and a paper cone in an unheated cabin — shore power makes a small dehumidifier cheap insurance. Elevate everything off cavity floors.

### Vibration & electrical hygiene
Amps never live un-isolated in the sub's cavity (documented failure mode) — separate rubber-isolated backer boards. Class-T within 7" of the battery positive; switch in the remote lead; BMS alarm at the helm; no inverter in the audio chain; verify the legacy 30A stereo breaker and Yellow/Black-16 / Red-16 circuits against the Regal color chart before reusing anything.

## 5. The Measurement Checklist (priority order)

**Bring: tape, moisture meter, bore-scope/phone camera, IR thermometer, a 24×24×27" cardboard mockup, and a level.**

1. **Access apertures (go/no-go for the whole sub plan):** clear width × height (and turn geometry) of the companionway/bi-fold door and the mid-cabin access opening. Walk the cardboard mockup through. This single test decides one-piece vs. two-piece gasketed vs. build-in-place — and whether the 19-20" frame driver itself passes.
2. **Mid-berth footwell:** L × W × H at the aft end of the berth; berth-deck-to-cockpit-sole height; flat mounting area on the ER bulkhead and hull sides for cleats/backer boards, clear of the white switch box.
3. **Fuel tank & drain channel:** locate the tank top and deck-to-tank clearance via the sender/inspection access; inspect and flow-test the centerline drain channel between fuel and waste tanks. No fabrication starts until this is done.
4. **Battery-switch area:** exact white-box position; clear flat area beside it for a Group-31-size LiFePO4 tray + Class-T + mono-block board; standoff depth before intruding on the mattress; **ER-bulkhead surface temp 30 min after a hard run + genset hour** (LiFePO4 charge ceiling check); confirm bulkhead penetrations are sealed (vapor-barrier status).
5. **High-current run lengths:** switch-box-to-mono-block actual cable path (must stay ≤8 ft one-way for 1/0; past ~10 ft, go 2/0); gland point through structure; run length to the sub.
6. **Helm cavity:** dimensions behind the dash/kick panels and how they remove; **peak under-dash temperature on a sunny afternoon with canvas up**; identify and gauge-check the existing Kenwood speaker runs, Yellow/Black-16 memory and Red-16 remote circuits, and any legacy 30A stereo breaker; measured chase length battery-site-to-helm (tape the messenger).
7. **Screen bulkhead:** flat area W × H vs. VESA footprint; pull the buttoned panels and **photograph everything behind** (harness, switch box, core thickness for bolt length); viewing distance/height from the settee (sets screen size); walkway clearance with the screen mounted; node-cavity depth (~3-4" with airflow) and cable path to the helm amp.
8. **Port-noise check:** distance from the planned port mouth to the sleeper's head; confirm room for a 60-80 in² flared slot.
9. **Settee base (fallback insurance):** internal dims, tallest clear face vs. the ~21" the frame needs, everything routed through it (table hardware, hoses, harness), and sole-to-water-tank clearance below (25-gal tank is centered under the cabin floor).
10. **Trim baseline:** measure the at-dock list, then simulate the build with ~150-250 lb of movable weight (patio pavers) at the planned stations and re-measure — cheap proof the port bias lands right.
11. **Phase-2 candidates (measure only to file away):** starboard cockpit step box internals, wall thickness, drainage, and what it opens into below (for a future port exit or 10-12" cockpit sub); wet-bar moisture readings (for the elimination record).

**Order of operations:** request the Regal drawings (Section 1) and run measurements 1-4 before buying wood or the driver recone budget gets committed to a box that can't board the boat.
